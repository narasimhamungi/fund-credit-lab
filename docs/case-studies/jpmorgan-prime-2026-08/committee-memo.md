# Committee memo: JPMorgan Prime Money Market Fund (practice)

**To:** Practice credit committee · **From:** Narasimha Mungi · **Date:** 30 September 2026
**Subject:** Profile review, data as of 31 August 2026 (SEC Form N-MFP)

> Independent illustration for practice. Not a credit rating and not any agency's methodology. Assessments use this
> project's own scale. Figures and sources: [rationale](rationale.md).

## Recommendation

Assess the credit profile as **Strong**, with **liquidity on watch**. Recheck when the September filing is available.

## Why

1. **Credit quality is high and well spread.**
   - 98.1% of net assets is rated at security level. Moody's rates 90.8% of net assets P-1.
   - The largest legal entity is 3.44% and the top five are 13.69%, both below the prime peer median.
2. **Maturity is comfortably within limits.** WAM is 42 days (limit 60) and WAL is 67 days (limit 120).
3. **Weekly liquidity is thin, but that is normal for prime funds.**
   - The fund's lowest reading was 50.30%.
   - Half of the 38 prime funds had a lowest reading at or below 52.74%.
   - The SEC 50% acquisition test is binding across the sector, and no day fell below it.

## Watch items

- **Daily liquidity: the lowest in the peer group.**
  - It fell to 26.23% on 31 August, the 5th percentile of peers against a median of 38.05%.
  - That was the same day the worst five-day net outflow ended: $3.17bn, or 3.4% of net assets.
  - A 30% instant redemption would exceed month-end daily liquid assets.
- **Non-government repo: 25.3% of net assets, the 87th percentile of peers.**
  - The collateral behind it is 51% corporate debt, 14% asset-backed securities, 11% equities and 10% private-label
    CMOs.
  - Overcollateralisation is about 6–8% on the largest positions.

## What would change the view

**Downward:**
- Weekly liquid assets held below 50%, or falling toward the 25% board-notification level.
- Repeated month-end daily liquidity below 25%.
- Non-government repo rising, or shifting toward equity and private-label collateral.
- Overcollateralisation below about 5%.

**Upward:** a larger month-end daily buffer, or less reliance on non-government repo.

## Questions for the committee

1. Is running daily liquidity at the 5th percentile a deliberate yield choice, and how does the manager fund
   month-end outflows?
2. What haircuts does the manager require by collateral type, and how are the equity and private-label positions
   valued in stress?
3. Does the 10%/30% liquidity range cited in published AAAmmf commentary still match the current MMF Rating Criteria?
   It must be confirmed before this memo relies on it.

## Limits

- One month of as-filed data.
- Concentration is at legal-entity level, so group exposure may be higher.
- Repo is shown gross of collateral.
- Governance and redemption terms are not assessed; the prospectus review is the next step.
